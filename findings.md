# Memo: Why HCR's Housing Vouchers Go Unused

**Basis:** NYS Comptroller audit 2022-S-28 (area data Apr 2017–Mar 2022), HUD Picture of Subsidized
Households 2017–2025, HUD FY2021 Fair Market Rents, Census ACS 2017–2021. Public data only. The
findings below are indicators, not proof of cause. Method and caveats are in README.md.

## 1. The biggest gaps come from vouchers that were never issued

In the six worst areas, HCR issued only **33–88%** of its available vouchers, yet **92–99%** of
families who received one leased a unit (audit Exhibit):

- Town of Hempstead: 33% issued
- Apple Meadow: 54%
- Westchester's Enhanced Section 8 Outreach Program: 74%
- Town of Eastchester: 77%
- Brooklyn ("New York East"): 82%
- "New York North": 88%

Families in these areas can find housing. The vouchers just aren't reaching them.

**What HCR could do:** Review the local administrators in these areas first: waiting-list management,
how often vouchers are pulled, staffing and processing times. Set issuance targets per area and
report against them monthly.

## 2. A smaller set of areas looks market-constrained

In **8 areas**, vouchers were issued but too many holders failed to lease, in markets with rental
vacancy under 5% or rents near or above the FMR:

- Schuyler (87% of holders leased; vacancy 3.2%)
- Bronx & Queens
- Steuben
- Franklin
- Nassau
- Chautauqua
- Suffolk
- Tompkins

**What HCR could do:** Raise payment standards toward 110% of FMR, or request exception payment
standards, in these areas. Extend search times. Add landlord incentives such as signing bonuses and
damage funds. Help with housing search. The audit notes HCR's administrative fee reserve of about
$131M could pay for this kind of help.

## 3. Upstate, HCR's shortfall is mostly a shared local problem

Where another housing agency is based in the same county, that agency used a *smaller* share of its
vouchers than HCR in **19 of 26** below-standard areas (HUD, 2017–2021). HCR clearly trails local
peers (by 5+ points) only in:

- Town of Hempstead (+46 points)
- Westchester's two special programs (+10 to +11)
- Columbia County (+9)
- Brooklyn (+6)

**What HCR could do:** Treat most upstate shortfalls as regional market and landlord problems, and
work on them with the local agencies, for example through shared landlord outreach and joint
inspections. Treat the five lagging areas as HCR-specific problems.

## 4. It has not improved since the audit

HUD reports HCR's statewide use at **91% in 2017 and 87% in 2025**. There was a brief recovery to 91%
in 2023. In 2025, about **6,800** vouchers sat unused. At HCR's 2025 average HUD subsidy of $1,580 per
month, that is roughly **$128 million a year** of rental help not reaching families. This is an
estimate and assumes full funding; the audit put available surplus at up to $36M in 2021.

**What HCR could do:** Answer the audit's first recommendation directly: publish an area-by-area
diagnosis of why vouchers go unused, using the issuance/leasing split above as the starting point,
and repeat it each year.

## 5. Many areas don't fit either explanation

**15 areas** are "mixed / unclear". Ten of them fall just short of 95% on several fronts, with no
single clear cause. Two had shortfalls in both issuance and leasing (Eastchester, Orleans). Three
lost holders during the housing search even though rents and vacancy look normal: Chemung, Lewis and
Sullivan, where 91–94% of holders leased. Price is unlikely to be the main cause in those three.
Landlord refusal, failed inspections or a thin rental stock are more likely.

**What HCR could do:** Track why searches fail in each area: landlord refusals, failed inspections,
time-outs. That data would turn this public-data indicator into an actual diagnosis.
